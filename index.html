Jamboree on the Air · JOTA-JOTI

Talk to Scouts around the world by radio!

Over 2 million Scouts get on the air on the third weekend of October. Scouts have been doing it since 1957.

The big idea

No phone. No Wi-Fi. No internet.
Just the air.

A phone or video call

Needs masts, cables, Wi-Fi and the internet in the middle. If the network is down, no call.

Ham radio

Needs two radios and two antennas. Radio waves carry your voice straight from our antenna to theirs.

Two kinds of radio

Broadcast radio vs ham radio

Broadcast radio (music & news stations)

One station sends. Everyone else only listens. Nobody can talk back.

Ham radio (radio amateurs & JOTA)

Both stations can talk and listen. We take turns, like a walkie-talkie.

The magic button

Press. Talk. Let go.

  1. 1

    Press and hold

    Push the button first, then start talking. The light goes to TX.

  2. 2

    Talk clearly

    Hold the mic about a hand-span (10 cm) from your mouth. Speak slowly.

  3. 3

    Let go to listen

    Say “over”, then release. You can't hear them while you hold the button!

The journey of your voice

1

You speak

Your voice makes the air wobble. That's sound.

2

Microphone

Turns the wobbles into a tiny electric signal.

3

Radio

Puts your voice onto a super-fast radio wave.

4

Antenna

Sends the wave flying through the air.

5

Their radio

Catches the wave, takes your voice off and plays it.

Radio waves travel at the speed of light. They could go round the Earth about 7 times in one second!
Inside the radio

Your voice rides a radio wave

Your voice

Slow, wobbly. Can't travel far on its own.

+

Radio wave

Super fast. Can travel a very long way.

=

Voice + wave

The wave's shape now carries your voice.

Like a delivery van

The radio wave is a super-fast van. Your voice is the parcel inside.

At the other end, their radio unpacks the parcel and plays your voice out of the speaker.

For grown-ups: changing the wave's height is AM, changing its frequency is FM. Hams use FM on VHF/UHF and SSB on HF.

Radio waves come in sizes

Long waves, short waves

HF3 – 30 MHz
10 – 100 m longFrom a bus to a football pitch!
VHF30 – 300 MHz
1 – 10 m longThe “2 metre” band: as tall as a door.
UHF300 – 3,000 MHz
10 cm – 1 m longThe “70 cm” band: about a grown-up's arm.

Longer waves travel further, like the boom of a bass drum you can hear right across a festival field.

VHF & UHF

Straight lines, like a torch beam

Best for: across town
Hills, buildings and the Earth’s curve block it
A repeater passes it on
HF

Bounces off the sky, round the world

Best for: other countries
The ionosphere bounces HF back down, like a mirror
The Sun powers it, so it changes from day to night
The radio alphabet

Spell it out: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie…

On a crackly radio, B, D, P and V all sound alike. Bravo, Delta, Papa and Victor don't!

What to say on air

Great to share

  • Your first name
  • Your age and Scout section
  • The weather, camps and games
  • Questions about their country

Keep private

  • Your surname
  • Your address or school
  • Phone numbers or emails
  • Politics or religion
